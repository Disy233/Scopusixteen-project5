Machine Learning Approaches for Predicting Protein Structures: A Review

Authors: A. Jana, R. K. Singh, P. Verma Published: May 20, 2026 DOI: 10.1234/jeas.2026.0142

Abstract

Protein structure prediction remains one of the central challenges in computational biology. Recent advances in machine learning, particularly deep learning architectures such as AlphaFold and related transformer-based models, have dramatically improved accuracy. This review surveys the major methodological developments, evaluates their performance on standard benchmarks, and discusses remaining limitations and future research directions.

1. Introduction

Understanding the three-dimensional structure of proteins is essential for elucidating biological function and for applications in drug discovery and biotechnology. Traditional experimental methods such as X-ray crystallography and NMR spectroscopy, while accurate, are time-consuming and expensive. Computational approaches have therefore become indispensable.

2. Classical Methods

Early computational methods relied on homology modeling, threading, and ab initio physical simulations. While useful for proteins with close homologs of known structure, these approaches struggled with novel folds and large multi-domain proteins.

3. Deep Learning Revolution

The introduction of deep residual networks and later attention-based models marked a turning point. AlphaFold 2 demonstrated near-experimental accuracy on a large fraction of CASP targets, fundamentally changing the field. Subsequent models have improved speed, confidence estimation, and the ability to handle complexes.

4. Current Challenges

Despite impressive progress, challenges remain in predicting conformational ensembles, protein-protein interfaces, and the effects of post-translational modifications. Integration with experimental data and improved uncertainty quantification are active areas of research.

5. Conclusion

Machine learning has transformed protein structure prediction. Continued advances in model architecture, training data, and hybrid experimental-computational pipelines promise further gains in both accuracy and biological insight.
