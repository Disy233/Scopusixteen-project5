RESEARCH INTEGRITY

Publication Ethics

Principles and procedures designed to promote responsible research, transparent scholarly communication and integrity throughout the publication process.

Policy status notice

This page provides a publication-ethics framework for the Scopusixteen Publishing website prototype. Before the platform operates as a real publisher, these policies should be formally adopted, reviewed and implemented by the responsible publisher and editorial teams.

Our Commitment to Research Integrity

Scopusixteen Publishing is designed around the principle that scholarly communication should be conducted with accuracy, transparency, fairness and respect for research participants, authors, reviewers, editors and readers.

Authors, editors, reviewers and publishing personnel should act responsibly throughout manuscript preparation, peer review, editorial decision-making, publication and post-publication activities.

Integrity Research and publication records should be accurate and trustworthy.
Transparency Relevant methods, funding, conflicts and declarations should be disclosed.
Fairness Editorial and peer-review decisions should be based on appropriate scholarly criteria.
Accountability Concerns about published research should be investigated through appropriate procedures.

1. Authors' Responsibilities

Authors are responsible for ensuring that manuscripts submitted for publication accurately represent their research and comply with applicable ethical and scholarly requirements.

  • Submit original and appropriately attributed work.
  • Provide accurate descriptions of methods, analyses and results.
  • Avoid fabrication, falsification and manipulation of research data.
  • Properly acknowledge sources and contributions.
  • Ensure that all listed authors have made appropriate contributions.
  • Obtain necessary research ethics approvals where applicable.
  • Disclose relevant conflicts of interest and funding.
  • Avoid submitting the same manuscript simultaneously to multiple journals unless expressly permitted.

2. Plagiarism and Improper Attribution

Plagiarism includes presenting another person's intellectual work, words, ideas, data, images or other material as one's own without appropriate attribution.

Manuscripts may be subject to appropriate similarity screening or editorial assessment. A similarity percentage by itself does not necessarily establish plagiarism; editors should consider the context and source material.

Where credible concerns arise, the publisher may seek clarification from authors and take appropriate editorial action.

3. Duplicate and Redundant Publication

Authors should not submit substantially identical manuscripts to multiple journals at the same time.

Material that substantially overlaps with previously published or submitted work should be transparently identified and explained.

4. Fabrication and Falsification

Fabrication refers to making up research data, observations or results. Falsification includes manipulating research materials, processes, images, datasets or results in a manner that misrepresents the research record.

Credible allegations of serious research misconduct should be assessed through an appropriate investigation process.

5. Authorship and Contributorship

Authorship should reflect meaningful scholarly contribution to the research and manuscript.

All authors should have an opportunity to review and approve the submitted version and should accept appropriate responsibility for their contribution.

Authorship disputes

If a dispute concerning authorship arises, editorial teams may request documentation or clarification. Where appropriate, unresolved disputes may need to be referred to the relevant institution or organization responsible for the research.

6. Conflicts of Interest

Authors, reviewers and editors should disclose relationships or circumstances that could reasonably be perceived as influencing their judgement.

A conflict does not necessarily mean that research cannot be published. Appropriate disclosure and management are important parts of transparent scholarly communication.

7. Funding and Financial Disclosure

Authors should disclose relevant sources of financial support for the research and, where appropriate, the role of funders in study design, data collection, analysis, interpretation and publication.

8. Research Ethics

Research involving human participants, animals, sensitive information or other regulated activities should comply with applicable ethical requirements and institutional or regulatory approvals.

Where relevant, manuscripts should include appropriate information about ethics approval, informed consent, participant privacy and other safeguards.

9. Data Integrity and Availability

Authors should maintain accurate research records and report methods and results honestly.

Where appropriate and ethically permissible, authors should provide information about data availability, supporting materials, datasets or analytical resources.

Privacy, confidentiality, intellectual property and applicable legal restrictions should be considered before research data are shared.

10. Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence tools may be used in scholarly workflows where permitted, but authors remain responsible for the accuracy, originality, integrity and appropriate attribution of their work.

  • AI systems should not be treated as accountable authors.
  • Authors should verify AI-generated information, references and claims.
  • Material use of AI tools should be disclosed when required by the applicable journal policy.
  • Authors should not upload confidential or sensitive research information to external AI services without appropriate authorization.

The final AI-use requirements should be specified in the formal policy adopted by each journal.

11. Reviewers' Responsibilities

  • Treat manuscripts as confidential documents.
  • Provide objective and constructive assessments.
  • Declare potential conflicts of interest.
  • Avoid using unpublished information for personal advantage.
  • Identify relevant concerns about methodology, originality or research integrity.
  • Complete reviews within agreed timelines where reasonably possible.

12. Editors' Responsibilities

Editors should make decisions based on scholarly considerations and should manage manuscripts fairly and confidentially.

  • Assess manuscripts appropriately.
  • Select suitable reviewers where external review is required.
  • Manage conflicts of interest.
  • Protect confidential manuscript information.
  • Investigate credible publication-ethics concerns.
  • Maintain appropriate separation between editorial decisions and commercial considerations.

13. Research and Publication Misconduct

Potential misconduct may include plagiarism, fabrication, falsification, inappropriate authorship, undisclosed conflicts, manipulation of peer review, duplicate publication or other serious breaches of research integrity.

Allegations should be handled carefully and fairly. Where necessary, authors may be given an opportunity to respond and relevant institutions may be contacted.

1
Concern received A potential integrity issue is reported or identified.
2
Preliminary assessment The concern is assessed to determine whether further action is appropriate.
3
Author response Relevant authors may be invited to provide clarification or supporting information.
4
Investigation or referral Serious matters may require additional investigation or institutional referral.
5
Editorial action Appropriate action may include correction, withdrawal, rejection or other measures.

14. Corrections, Retractions and Withdrawals

The integrity of the scholarly record is important. Where a published article contains a significant error or integrity concern, an appropriate post-publication notice may be considered.

Corrections

Minor or significant errors that do not invalidate the overall research may be addressed through an appropriate correction.

Retractions

A retraction may be appropriate where serious errors, unreliable findings or research-integrity concerns materially affect the publication record.

Withdrawals

Before publication, a manuscript may be withdrawn according to the applicable journal and publisher procedures.

15. Complaints and Appeals

Authors may raise legitimate concerns regarding editorial decisions or publication processes through the publisher's designated complaint or appeal procedure.

Complaints should provide sufficient information for the matter to be assessed fairly. The final complaint and appeals process should be established by the responsible publisher and editorial leadership.

16. Post-Publication Concerns

Readers and researchers may raise concerns about a published article when they identify credible issues relating to accuracy, research integrity, authorship, conflicts or other matters affecting the scholarly record.

Such concerns should be reviewed responsibly and, where necessary, addressed through an appropriate correction, editorial notice, investigation or other action.

17. Ethics Contact

A production publisher should provide a monitored contact channel for publication-ethics concerns, research-integrity reports and formal complaints.
