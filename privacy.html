PRIVACY & DATA PROTECTION

Privacy Policy

Information about how Scopusixteen Publishing may handle information when users interact with the website and its publishing services.

Prototype notice

This Privacy Policy is a website prototype. Some services described below, including user accounts, databases, payment processing, analytics and AI services, may not yet be connected to the live website. This policy should be reviewed and updated before the platform begins collecting personal information at scale.

1. Introduction

Scopusixteen Publishing ("Scopusixteen", "we", "us" or "our") respects the privacy of individuals who use our website, publishing platform and related services.

This Privacy Policy explains the types of information that may be collected, how information may be used, how it may be protected and the choices available to users.

By using the website, users should review this policy together with the applicable Terms and Conditions and other publishing policies.

2. Information We May Collect

Depending on which services are implemented and used, the platform may process several categories of information.

Account information

  • Name.
  • Email address.
  • Organisation or institutional affiliation.
  • Account credentials and authentication data.
  • Profile information supplied by the user.

Manuscript and publication information

  • Manuscript titles.
  • Author and contributor information.
  • Affiliations.
  • Abstracts and keywords.
  • Manuscript files and supplementary materials.
  • Editorial correspondence.
  • Reviewer and editorial information where applicable.

Technical information

  • IP address or approximate network information.
  • Browser and device information.
  • Operating system.
  • Pages visited and interaction information.
  • Security and diagnostic information.

3. How Information May Be Used

Information may be used for legitimate operational, publishing, security and service-related purposes, including:

  • Creating and managing user accounts.
  • Processing manuscript submissions.
  • Communicating with authors.
  • Managing editorial workflows.
  • Facilitating peer review.
  • Providing publishing and subscription services.
  • Processing legitimate payments where payment services are implemented.
  • Preventing fraud, abuse and unauthorised access.
  • Improving website performance and usability.
  • Responding to support enquiries.
  • Complying with applicable legal obligations.

4. Manuscript Confidentiality

Manuscripts submitted for editorial consideration may contain unpublished research, intellectual property, personal information and confidential material.

Access to manuscript information should therefore be restricted to authorised individuals who require the information for legitimate editorial, peer-review, production, administrative or technical purposes.

The exact confidentiality controls will depend on the technical infrastructure and editorial procedures implemented by the platform.

5. AI Assistant and AI Services

Scopusixteen may provide AI-assisted tools intended to help users navigate information, prepare submissions, identify relevant resources or perform other permitted editorial-support functions.

The current website prototype does not establish that AI conversations or manuscript data are being sent to a live artificial-intelligence service.

Before a live AI service is connected, the platform should establish clear rules concerning what information may be submitted, how information is processed, retention periods and applicable third-party providers.

Important for authors

Authors should avoid entering confidential, sensitive or unpublished manuscript material into an AI tool unless the applicable publishing policy expressly permits such use and appropriate data protections are in place.

6. Cookies and Similar Technologies

The website may use cookies or similar technologies when such functionality is implemented.

Cookies may be used for purposes such as:

  • Remembering website preferences.
  • Maintaining secure sessions.
  • Measuring website performance.
  • Understanding general usage patterns.

Any analytics, advertising or non-essential tracking technologies should be disclosed and configured in accordance with applicable requirements.

7. Payment Information

If subscriptions, APCs or other paid services are introduced, payment information may be processed through approved third-party payment providers.

Where possible, sensitive payment card information should be handled directly by the payment provider rather than stored unnecessarily on the publishing platform.

The current subscription and payment interfaces on the prototype website do not by themselves establish that a real payment transaction has occurred.

8. Third-Party Services

The platform may eventually rely on third-party providers for services such as:

  • Website hosting.
  • Authentication.
  • Database infrastructure.
  • Payment processing.
  • Email delivery.
  • AI services.
  • Analytics and security.

When such providers are connected, the platform should maintain an appropriate list of providers and disclose relevant data-sharing arrangements where required.

9. Data Retention

Personal information should generally be retained only for as long as reasonably necessary for the purpose for which it was collected, unless a longer retention period is required or permitted by law.

Manuscripts, editorial records, publication records and financial records may have different retention requirements.

A formal retention schedule should be established before the production platform becomes operational.

10. Data Security

Reasonable technical and organisational measures should be used to protect information against unauthorised access, alteration, disclosure, loss or destruction.

Depending on the final platform architecture, these measures may include:

  • Secure authentication.
  • Encryption in transit.
  • Access controls.
  • Administrative permissions.
  • Security monitoring.
  • Backups and recovery procedures.

No internet-based system can guarantee absolute security.

11. User Privacy Rights

Depending on the user's location and applicable law, individuals may have rights concerning their personal information.

These may include rights to:

  • Request access to personal information.
  • Request correction of inaccurate information.
  • Request deletion where legally applicable.
  • Object to certain processing.
  • Request restriction of certain processing.
  • Withdraw consent where processing relies on consent.

Requests should be assessed according to the applicable legal requirements and the circumstances of the request.

12. Children's Privacy

The publishing platform is intended primarily for researchers, authors, reviewers, editors, institutions and other academic users.

The platform should not knowingly collect personal information from children where prohibited by applicable law.

13. International Data Processing

Academic publishing may involve authors, reviewers, editors, institutions and service providers located in different countries.

If information is transferred internationally, the applicable production system should implement the required safeguards and provide appropriate disclosures.

14. External Websites

The website may contain links to external websites or services. Scopusixteen is not responsible for the privacy practices of independent third-party websites.

Users should review the privacy policies of external services before providing personal information.

15. Changes to This Policy

This Privacy Policy may be updated when the website, publishing services, legal requirements or technical infrastructure changes.

The latest version should be published on this page with an appropriate effective date.

Current status

Prototype policy — formal legal review and operational implementation should take place before production launch.

Privacy Enquiries

For privacy-related questions or requests, contact the publishing support team through the official contact channel once the production contact system is active.
