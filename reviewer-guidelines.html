Reviewer Guidelines

Guidance for conducting constructive, confidential and ethically responsible peer reviews for manuscripts submitted to Scopusixteen Publishing journals.

Individual journals may adopt additional requirements, review models, deadlines or subject-specific instructions. The live policy should be reviewed and approved before being used as an operational publishing policy.

Core Principles of Peer Review

Reviewers are expected to approach every assignment professionally and independently.

I

Impartiality

Evaluate the manuscript on its scholarly merits rather than the identity, institution, nationality or personal characteristics of the authors.

C

Confidentiality

Treat manuscript content, reviewer discussions and editorial materials as confidential.

R

Respect

Provide professional and constructive criticism focused on improving the scholarly work.

Before Accepting a Review

Before accepting an invitation, reviewers should consider whether they have sufficient expertise, time and independence to complete the assessment.

Consider the following:

  • Do you have appropriate subject or methodological expertise?
  • Can you complete the review within the requested timeframe?
  • Do you have any conflict of interest with the authors, institutions or research?
  • Are you able to provide an objective and professional assessment?
  • Have you previously reviewed, collaborated with or directly supervised any of the authors?

If a significant conflict exists, the reviewer should decline the invitation or contact the responsible editor for guidance.

Conflicts of Interest

A conflict of interest may arise when a reviewer has a relationship or competing interest that could reasonably affect, or appear to affect, the objectivity of the review.

Examples may include:

  • Recent collaboration with one or more authors.
  • Current employment or close professional relationships with an author.
  • Personal relationships that could influence judgement.
  • Direct financial or commercial interests related to the manuscript.
  • Competitive research interests where confidential information could create an unfair advantage.

When uncertain, disclose the situation to the editor rather than making the decision privately.

Confidentiality

Manuscripts submitted for peer review are confidential scholarly materials. Reviewers should not disclose or use unpublished information obtained through the review process.

  • Do not share the manuscript with unauthorised individuals.
  • Do not post manuscript content publicly.
  • Do not use unpublished ideas or results for personal research advantage.
  • Do not contact authors directly about the manuscript unless the journal explicitly permits it.
  • Destroy or appropriately delete downloaded review materials when they are no longer required, subject to the journal's procedures.

How to Evaluate a Manuscript

A useful review considers both the strengths and limitations of the manuscript.

1. Research Question

Consider whether the research problem is clearly defined and appropriate for the journal.

  • Is the research question clear?
  • Is the purpose adequately explained?
  • Does the study address a meaningful problem?

2. Originality & Contribution

Assess whether the manuscript provides a meaningful contribution to existing knowledge.

  • Does it offer new findings or perspectives?
  • Is the contribution clearly explained?
  • Is the work sufficiently differentiated from prior research?

3. Methodology

Evaluate whether the methods are appropriate, sufficiently described and logically applied.

  • Are the methods suitable?
  • Is the sample or data adequately described?
  • Are analytical procedures appropriate?

4. Results

Assess whether the results are presented clearly and supported by the evidence.

  • Are findings clearly reported?
  • Do results address the research question?
  • Are tables and figures appropriate?

5. Discussion

Consider whether the authors interpret the findings appropriately.

  • Are findings compared with previous research?
  • Are limitations acknowledged?
  • Are conclusions supported by evidence?

6. Presentation

Assess the organisation, readability, references and overall scholarly presentation.

  • Is the manuscript logically structured?
  • Is the language sufficiently clear?
  • Are citations and references appropriate?

Writing a Constructive Review

✓ Recommended

  • Explain the reasoning behind major criticisms.
  • Identify specific sections that require clarification.
  • Distinguish major methodological problems from minor presentation issues.
  • Suggest realistic improvements where appropriate.
  • Recognise significant strengths as well as weaknesses.

✕ Avoid

  • Personal attacks or disrespectful language.
  • Comments about an author's nationality, identity or personal characteristics.
  • Unsubstantiated accusations of misconduct.
  • Demanding citations solely to increase a reviewer's own citation count.
  • Recommending rejection without explaining the substantive scholarly reasons.

Structuring the Reviewer Report

A clear report helps editors make decisions and gives authors useful information for improving their manuscript.

Overall Assessment Briefly summarise the manuscript's contribution, strengths and principal concerns.
Major Comments Explain substantive issues involving research design, evidence, analysis or interpretation.
Minor Comments Identify smaller issues involving clarity, organisation, terminology or presentation.

Comments to Authors and Editors

Comments to Authors

These comments should be professional, constructive and sufficiently detailed to help authors understand what should be improved.

Confidential Comments to the Editor

Where the review system provides a confidential editor field, use it for information that should not be communicated directly to authors. Keep these comments professional and consistent with the overall recommendation.

Reviewers should avoid making contradictory recommendations across the two sections.

Editorial Recommendations

The final recommendation should reflect the evidence presented in the review.

Accept
The manuscript is suitable for publication with no substantive changes required, subject to editorial checks.
Minor Revision
The manuscript is potentially publishable after relatively limited corrections or clarifications.
Major Revision
Substantive revisions are required before the manuscript can be considered further.
Reject & Resubmit
The current manuscript has substantial limitations, but a substantially revised submission may be considered where journal policy permits.
Reject
The manuscript is not suitable for publication in its current form based on substantive scholarly, methodological or journal-scope considerations.

Responsible Use of AI During Peer Review

Protect Confidential Manuscripts

Reviewers should not upload confidential manuscripts, unpublished research or identifying information to external AI systems unless the journal expressly permits such use and appropriate confidentiality safeguards are in place.

  • Do not use AI to replace your own scholarly judgement.
  • Verify any AI-generated information before relying on it.
  • Do not disclose confidential manuscript content to unauthorised third-party services.
  • Follow the journal's specific AI and reviewer policy.

Review Deadlines

Reviewers should aim to complete accepted assignments within the deadline provided by the editorial system.

  • Accept an invitation only when you reasonably expect to meet the deadline.
  • Contact the editor promptly if circumstances change.
  • Request an extension rather than submitting an incomplete review without explanation.
  • Decline future invitations if your workload prevents timely reviews.

Reviewer Ethics & Misconduct

Concerns about reviewer conduct may include breaches of confidentiality, misuse of unpublished information, conflicts that were not disclosed, inappropriate communications or other conduct inconsistent with responsible peer review.

Concerns should be referred to the responsible editorial or publication-ethics contact for assessment under the applicable journal procedures.
