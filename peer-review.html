Peer Review Process

Our editorial workflow is designed to support rigorous, fair and transparent evaluation of scholarly manuscripts.

Policy notice: The workflow described on this page represents the intended Scopusixteen Publishing editorial framework. Individual journals may adapt the process according to their discipline and specific editorial policy.

Overview

Manuscripts submitted to a Scopusixteen journal may undergo editorial assessment and peer review before a publication decision is reached.

Peer review helps editors and authors assess the quality, originality, relevance, methodological soundness and scholarly contribution of submitted research.

Editorial Workflow

1
Manuscript Submission The corresponding author submits the manuscript and required information through the submission system.
2
Initial Editorial Screening The editorial team checks the manuscript for basic suitability, completeness, scope and compliance with submission requirements.
3
Reviewer Selection Where peer review is required, appropriate reviewers may be invited based on subject expertise and potential conflicts of interest.
4
Peer Review Reviewers evaluate the manuscript according to the journal's criteria and provide comments and recommendations.
5
Editorial Decision The editor considers the reviewers' reports together with the manuscript and makes an appropriate editorial decision.
6
Revision Where revisions are requested, authors may submit a revised manuscript and response to reviewers.
7
Final Decision The editor issues the final decision after considering the available evidence and any required revisions.
8
Production and Publication Accepted manuscripts may proceed to copyediting, typesetting, metadata preparation and publication according to the journal's workflow.

Peer Review Models

Journals may use different review models. The applicable model should be clearly stated by each journal.

Single-Blind Review

Reviewer identities are concealed from authors while reviewer comments may be shared with authors.

Double-Blind Review

Reviewer and author identities are concealed from each other where practical.

Open Review

Reviewer and author identities may be disclosed according to the journal's policy.

Editorial Review

Certain article types may be assessed primarily through editorial review where the journal permits it.

What Reviewers May Consider

  • Relevance to the journal's aims and scope
  • Originality and contribution to knowledge
  • Research question or objectives
  • Methodological quality
  • Accuracy and clarity of analysis
  • Presentation of results
  • Quality of discussion and conclusions
  • Appropriate use of references
  • Ethical considerations
  • Overall clarity and scholarly quality

Possible Editorial Decisions

Accept The manuscript is suitable for publication subject to any final production requirements.
Minor Revision Limited changes are required before a final decision can be made.
Major Revision Substantial changes are required and the revised manuscript may undergo additional review.
Reject and Resubmit The current submission is not suitable for acceptance, but a substantially revised manuscript may be considered where the journal permits.
Reject The manuscript is not suitable for publication in the journal.

Responsibilities of Authors

  • Submit original work.
  • Provide accurate information.
  • Respond appropriately to reviewer comments.
  • Identify changes made in a revised manuscript where requested.
  • Disclose relevant conflicts of interest.
  • Correct significant errors discovered after submission or publication.

Responsibilities of Reviewers

  • Evaluate manuscripts objectively.
  • Maintain confidentiality.
  • Identify potential conflicts of interest.
  • Provide constructive and evidence-based feedback.
  • Avoid inappropriate personal criticism.
  • Alert editors to serious concerns about originality or research integrity.

Confidentiality

Manuscripts submitted for peer review should be treated as confidential. Reviewers should not share manuscript content or use unpublished information for personal advantage.

Editorial and review communications should be handled through the authorised publishing workflow.

Appeals and Complaints

Authors who believe that an editorial decision involved a significant procedural error may contact the relevant editorial office with a clear explanation and supporting information.

An appeal does not automatically result in reversal of an editorial decision.
